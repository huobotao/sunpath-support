This policy explains how SunArc uses the camera, location and device orientation to display a reference sun path, and what data is involved in local caching, website visits and support emails.

Calculations on your device

Sun positions, paths for the selected date and screen overlays are calculated on your device. This version has no developer account registration or sign-in, advertising, behavioural analytics or developer server. It has no feature that sends camera images, location, device orientation or adjustment values to the developer.

The app itself does not integrate maps, cloud synchronisation or third-party analytics services. System services such as device location, and system backup and restoration, are managed by Apple and your system settings. This policy does not mean that your device or these system services never use a network.

Camera: live preview only

SunArc uses the live rear-camera view as a background and overlays the calculated sun path. This version has no feature for taking photos, recording video or audio, saving images to the photo library, or uploading images. It does not request microphone or photo-library access.

You can leave “Allow Camera” untapped or deny the system request. Without camera permission, the app cannot show the overlay on the live view and displays a permission explanation instead. You can change access through the app’s “Open Settings” control or in system settings. The app reads the camera permission again when it returns to the foreground.

Location and the local location cache

The app requests location access while in use to obtain latitude and longitude for sun-path calculations. After receiving a valid location, it saves the coordinates, location-sample timestamp and system-reported horizontal accuracy estimate in the app’s local data for use the next time you open it. It keeps the most recent valid location, not a journey log or list of past locations.

The interface distinguishes “Location”, “Cached location”, “Old location” and “Sydney (default)”, and shows the update time and accuracy estimate when available. A sample from within the last five minutes may be marked as recently updated. This time label does not guarantee that you are still at that location, and an accuracy estimate is not a measurement guarantee. Caches left by an older version may lack a timestamp or accuracy information; the app identifies the time as unknown.

You can deny location access or revoke it later in system settings. If no valid new location is obtained, an update fails or permission is unavailable, the app retains and labels its existing cached location. It uses the default Sydney coordinates if no usable cache exists. Revoking permission does not automatically delete coordinates already saved on the device or turn a cached location into your current location.

Device orientation and azimuth adjustment

The app reads orientation information from the device’s motion sensors to estimate the camera’s direction and elevation angle. It attempts to use a true-north reference and may use a magnetic-north reference when true north is unavailable. Orientation data is used for the current display; the app does not save it as a motion history or send it to the developer.

The angular offset set through “Adjust bearing” is saved separately on your device for later displays. “Reset” only returns this offset to zero. It does not delete the location cache or calibrate the hardware sensors.

Permissions, cache and removing data

You can manage SunArc’s camera and location permissions in your iPhone’s system settings. Denying camera access does not deny location access; check each permission separately. The app has no separate button for clearing the location cache, no developer account, and no interface through which the developer can remotely read or delete the local cache.

The system’s “Delete App” option removes the app and its local data, while “Offload App” keeps documents and data (see Apple’s explanation). Manage system backups separately.

This policy, support pages and website hosting

When you choose to open a policy or support page hosted on GitHub Pages, your browser requests the page from GitHub. GitHub states that GitHub Pages logs and stores visitors’ IP addresses for security purposes, even when they are not signed in to GitHub. Visiting a website is a different data-processing activity from SunArc’s sun-path calculations on your device.

These two pages contain no developer-added analytics scripts, remote fonts, advertising, forms or other automatically loaded third-party assets. If you follow an external link, that website’s own rules apply. See the GitHub Pages data-collection information and GitHub Privacy Statement.

Support emails you choose to send

Support email: huobotao@hotmail.com. Selecting the email link only asks your device to open an email app; it does not send an email automatically.

If you choose to send an email, your sender address, message and any attachments you add are processed by the email service and support recipient to respond to your question. The Hotmail mailbox uses Microsoft’s email service, and the services involved also process emails under their own rules. Support emails are not data automatically uploaded by the app.

Please do not send passwords, verification codes or unrelated private information. Troubleshooting usually does not require precise coordinates or original camera images. If a screenshot is needed, first hide coordinates and private surroundings. You can use the same email address to ask about or request deletion of support emails you previously sent; email-service and backup handling remains subject to those services’ mechanisms.
